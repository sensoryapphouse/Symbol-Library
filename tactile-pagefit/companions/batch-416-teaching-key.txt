Neutral helium-4: introduced shell and nucleus count model

This single teaching meaning is a declared neutral helium-4 atom in its ground state. It has two protons, two neutrons and two electrons. Atomic number two gives two protons; chosen mass number four gives two neutrons. Neutrality gives two electrons. NIST lists neutral helium ground-state configuration 1s squared: one occupied principal shell. Its tabulated standard atomic weight 4.002602 is not the integer mass number used to count nucleons.

An unfilled circle denotes a proton with positive charge; an unfilled rounded square denotes an electrically neutral neutron; a smaller unfilled diamond denotes an electron with negative charge. These are introduced tactile/count symbols, not the particles' actual outlines. Two circles and two rounded squares form an enlarged central nuclear cluster. Their separation permits counting and does not represent nuclear packing, a vacuum or forces. The two electron diamonds lie inside the single large circular shell guide, away from the nuclear cluster. The large circle is a nonliteral shell-occupancy guide, not another particle, the atom's hard boundary or an electron's trajectory. The two diametric marker positions are layout choices, not simultaneous physical electron positions, spin or motion. Read six particle glyphs: four nuclear and two electronic. Never count the ordinary Index line-sampling dots as particles.

Two positive protons balance the charge of two negative electrons; neutrons are neutral. A helium-4 nucleus alone would be an alpha particle, not the selected neutral atom. Helium-3, charged helium and extra occupied shells are outside this selected model. NIST He II is a distinct ion, not an extra shell in this picture.
