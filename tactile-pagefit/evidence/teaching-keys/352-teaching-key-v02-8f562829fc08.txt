1. SIX-CELL GLOBAL ATMOSPHERIC CIRCULATION
Orient the large latitude-height section with its two arrowed axes: horizontal from South (left) to North (right), and height upward. This is a meridional latitude-height section, NOT a surface-wind map, longitude diagram or depiction of Coriolis deflection. Seven short stations on the horizontal baseline stand, left to right, for South Pole, 60 degrees South, 30 degrees South, equator, 30 degrees North, 60 degrees North, North Pole. Their numbers/names appear only in this external key. Each loop is slightly inset from its illustrative interval; branches near a latitude are not exact coordinate measurements.
Read six idealised mean circulation loops, left to right: South Polar, South Ferrel, South Hadley, North Hadley, North Ferrel, North Polar. Follow each lower arrow and rising-branch arrow, continue along its upper return, and descend on the other side. South lower directions are right/left/right; North directions are left/right/left. Thus lower Hadley flow is equatorward, lower Ferrel flow poleward, and lower Polar flow equatorward. Rising branches lie near the equator and the two 60-degree stations; descending branches lie near the two 30-degree stations and poles. Three loops in each hemisphere mirror the other three.
Rectangular closed routes and equal illustrative widths/heights are deliberate schematic conventions, not rigid air pipes, precise air trajectories, permanent boundaries, altitude scales, measured flow strengths, local weather forecasts or proof of actual atmospheric motion. NOAA's ideal circulation model supports the selected relationships; native1025 cannot certify every source arrow.

2. ATMOSPHERIC TEMPERATURE–HEIGHT PROFILE
The vertical axis points upward for increasing height; the horizontal axis points right for increasing temperature. Three horizontal guides mark qualitative thermal-layer transitions. The continuous four-segment profile begins on the lower baseline and runs upward: temperature decreases through the troposphere, increases through the stratosphere, decreases through the mesosphere, then increases in the thermosphere. Its exact contacts with the three guides are part of the introduced model. The cornered reversals are illustrative, not measured source curves. The final upper endpoint is a selected plotting-window limit, not the top of the atmosphere.
No visible numeric ticks, quantitative temperature scale, universal boundary heights, actual local sounding or source1215 numerical readings are claimed. Real thermal boundaries vary. High thermospheric kinetic temperature does not mean dense hot air.

3. GREENHOUSE RADIATION-EXCHANGE PLAN
Read the upper-left rounded marker as the Sun; the lower horizontal line as the ground/surface. Four open corner brackets delimit a nonprinted atmospheric gas-region reference. The middle rounded marker is an abstract absorption/re-emission interaction reference within that region, NOT a molecule, container, solid glass lid, mirror, measured solar disc or literal material wall.
The incoming solar direction chevron lies at the midpoint of its shaft; the shaft continues to the surface. This deliberate inline-arrow adaptation separates sampling around the reflection junction without changing solar endpoints or meaning. Six externally keyed arrow paths are distinct: (a) the left descending path from the Sun reaches the surface: selected incoming solar energy; (b) the path departing that same surface location toward upper middle-left is partial solar reflection toward space; (c) a separate rising path from the middle surface reaches the bottom of the interaction marker: terrestrial surface emission absorbed by the atmosphere; (d) the marker's upward path represents atmospheric thermal re-emission toward space; (e) its right-hand downward path reaches the surface and represents downward atmospheric thermal re-emission; (f) the far-right rising surface path represents some terrestrial radiation escaping directly without the shown gas interaction.
Radiation roles are given by this key and path positions/directions, not intrinsic grayscale colour or an unmeasured wavelength code. No decorative rays, measured flux widths, quantitative energy budget or closed cyclic energy system are shown. Gases absorb and re-emit; the atmosphere does NOT simply bounce all heat back. The bracket region is a reference domain, not raised filled matter or empty physical air. Drawn contacts indicate the selected diagram's path/interaction relations, not calibrated physical particle collisions.
