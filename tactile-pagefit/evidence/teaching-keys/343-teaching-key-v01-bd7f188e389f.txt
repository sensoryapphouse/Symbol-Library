ESSENTIAL TEACHING KEY — THREE LEARNED HAZARD PICTOGRAMS
Batch 343. Keep this key with all three contour models and all medium variants.

These are educational recognition and discussion models of learned pictograms. They are not certified, operational or regulation-compliant labels; they do not test any substance, prove a real hazard, replace product information, or establish a learner recognition result. The three fixed meanings are:
1. Aquatic environment: “The learned tree/fish environmental-hazard pictogram.”
2. Flame: “The learned flame hazard mark.”
3. Oxidizer: “The learned flame-over-circle mark for oxidising hazards.”
The diamond is an introduced common frame, not another hazard meaning. No source caption, numeral, font, Braille, product logo or official colour specification is reproduced. Black contours on white backgrounds are a declared tactile teaching adaptation from source-filled motifs; they are not a certified replacement for a red-border label.

AQUATIC ENVIRONMENT MODEL
Within the diamond, find the one horizontal ground/water context line, the bare tree above it on the right and the fish below it. The vertical trunk joins the context line and four bare branches join the trunk. These are one tree, not multiple plants, leaves or extra animals. The fish has an elliptical body, a closed triangular tail at its left end and an introduced trapezoid lower-fin contour attached at two actual body points. Its two crossing eye strokes are a taught dead-fish motif, not a printed alphabetic label or an independent point symbol. There are no decorative waves or extra animals.
The model teaches the selected aquatic-environment hazard convention. It does not assert every ecological condition, identify a pollutant or species, or show evidence of actual animal or plant death. The horizontal line is a schematic context, not a surveyed shoreline or measured water level.

PLAIN FLAME MODEL
Inside the diamond is one flame outline, with outer tongues, a nested inner-void boundary and an integral flat lower base. There is NO circular component beneath it. The nested contour is taught as the flame's inner void in this outline variant. The intervening region remains unprinted in the line/dot versions: neither that white paper nor the reference domain is a measured physical air pocket, raised black solid or actual burning material. This is the learned plain flame mark, not a product logo, a test of flammability or a universal rule for every material or context.

FLAME-OVER-CIRCLE OXIDIZER MODEL
The flame contour above the circular component meets its top at one actual native point. The full circle is a distinguishing coded component and must not be omitted or confused with the plain flame model. The circle is not a planet, an oxygen molecule or an apparatus drawing. A 30 mm vertical support below the circle joins the horizontal supporting baseline. This support is an explicitly introduced tactile geometry adaptation; it avoids duplicated, nearly tangent dot traces and is not a claim of exact original 1664 proportions or official artwork. The flame, circle, support, base and diamond belong to one taught pictogram. The model does not imply every oxygen source or material has this hazard or establish any chemical performance.
