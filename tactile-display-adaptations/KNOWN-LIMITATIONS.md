# Known limitations and teaching requirements

These are experimental curriculum materials for evaluation. Whole individual exported profiles were digitally inspected by the author, with observations and image hashes in the catalogue. They have not received independent tactile proofreading, actual-device calibration, touch testing or learner recognition testing. Digital inspection does not establish BANA certification or suitability for every child.

All 42 meanings need their external teaching keys. The device's Braille/text controls or a teacher must supply titles, part names, sequence, orientation, quantities and scale changes. No wording or Braille is embedded in the pin graphics. Small hollow codes may be visually distinct yet difficult by touch; validate them on the device. Grid-local doubled pins at turns and intentional joins do not represent extra height levels or a separate material texture.

- Atomic shells are presented as countable electron groups, not literal spatial electron positions or physical shell sizes.
- Flags retain layout/count information across overview and detail frames. Colour requires the key; the binary images do not communicate colour by themselves. Multipart star sequences are not one complete flag surface.
- Planet/moon diameters, rings, electromagnetic bands and related quantitative companions rely on explicit quantities or scale explanations. Do not compare differently scaled frames as if they share one ruler.
- Gears include tooth-count and pitch references rather than a manufacturing-ready tooth-contact model.
- Forelimb homology uses labelled bone-count carriers. Its boxes do not show anatomical contours, relative lengths, joint articulation or a realistic animal silhouette.
- Dentition preserves the selected source example and local tooth sequence, not a universal species dental formula. Enlarged crown details and simplified jaw context must be read together.
- Chemistry uses atom/bond codes and keyed stereochemical conventions. Polymer boxes indicate connectivity, not a particular full-chain length or conformation. The maltose reducing-end configuration is the selected source example, not a claim that mutarotation is absent.
- The corrosion hazard is a multipart teaching aid, not a replacement for the official single safety pictogram on a product.
- The Vitruvian views preserve source body/frame proportions with enlarged contact details. Hand/foot carriers are not detailed anatomical drawings.
- Historical maps preserve selected source itineraries, not exact surveyed ship tracks or undisputed segment-by-segment history. Modern partial coastline references are not medieval geography. Introduced connector bends are not additional documented stops. Enlarged point codes represent locations, not area extents.
- Map layers share registration within each keyed group. Switch between them; do not overlay all layers, which would recreate ambiguous contacts on the pin grid. Regional windows may show edge continuations rather than complete objects. `source_window` rectangles are centred layout focuses; the catalogue also records the actual pin-origin coordinate extent. Small raster bends are generalised and the relevant tolerance/omissions are recorded. Marco Polo uses separate coast fragments and shared-endpoint continuations to avoid false closures; very sparse layers are context only, not standalone recognisable maps.

Per-meaning adaptation notes, acceptance requirements, per-frame teaching keys and observations in `catalogue.json` provide the detailed limitations. Physical evaluation may require further revision or rejection of an individual adaptation. The original paper and page-fit versions remain available unchanged.
