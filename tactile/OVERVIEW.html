Retained collection overview · 4 November 2026 · 898 meanings

Experimental tactile graphics for curriculum access

This project explores how independently authored, editable tactile graphics might help learners access concepts and relationships encountered in school and everyday learning. It is an experimental resource collection, not a validated teaching programme or a guarantee that every included topic is needed by every school.

ViewPlus shading update — 6 November 2026

163 ViewPlus variants now include selective area shading. The Index and swell-paper exports are unchanged. Human review of these shading revisions and physical test printing remain pending; the earlier retained decisions apply to the earlier artwork. See the assessment and printing guidance.

Purpose

The collection is intended to make selected spatial, structural and comparative information available for tactile exploration: following a route, comparing limb structures, locating organs, reading a graph or relating parts to a whole. Its value depends on the learner, the teaching task, the explanation and the physical result. The graphics should support purposeful instruction alongside real objects, models, discussion and other accessible materials.

Where the subjects came from

The project combined foundational and relational teaching targets with a broad, source-led survey of educational graphic subjects. Project records identify APH’s Tactile Graphics Image Library, as indexed publicly through BTactile, as a major topic guide. The recorded survey contained 1642 public catalogue records; it was not a complete current APH export, and catalogue records, views and titles were not treated as equivalent to distinct teaching meanings. The fixed scope was reconciled to 1251 named tactile meanings.

The catalogue helped identify subjects; it did not supply a verified curriculum requirement for each item. Per-item work uses retained source references and, where necessary, additional scientific, historical, anatomical or cartographic evidence to define a teachable model. New editable SVG geometry is independently authored under the project process. That does not imply complete reproduction of the reference image, ownership of source material, permission to redistribute source assets, or endorsement by APH, BTactile or an awarding body.

The earlier topic framework also recorded an IAGD public geoscience repository as a complementary reference resource. A framework reference does not mean every final diagram used that repository. The actual source lineage belongs in each item’s records.

Scope and formats

The original catalogue contains 1251 distinct meanings. This package contains the final 898 retained meanings: 856 previously reviewed in production and 42 kept by the human reviewer. Each retained meaning has one tagged editable master and three exports: swell paper, Index embossing and a provisional ViewPlus variable-height profile. That is 898 masters and 2694 profile exports. The combined package also contains the separate 1000-meaning visual AAC collection.

The historical curriculum audit considered 418 then-remaining definitions: 127 clear, 230 conditional and 61 questionable. The human removed the 291 conditional/questionable items, then excluded 62 more in the final review. The simplified sunflower v02 was kept. All 898 retained meanings are delivered. The later ViewPlus shading revisions need human review; physical evaluation is still pending.

Relationship to the curriculum

Some subjects have direct school objectives, such as cell structures, wave relationships, map reading and skeletal comparisons. Others are useful only within a selected course, locality or individual learning plan. Some catalogue-derived topics have weak demonstrated priority at their current specificity. The accompanying audit identifies these differences rather than describing every item as curriculum-required.

The audit uses England’s KS1–4 programmes and selected GCSE/A-level specifications, with targeted checks of Montana, Louisiana and Utah sources. It is not an exhaustive review of all school systems. Mapping a topic to an objective is our editorial judgement; it does not mean that the standard requires this particular image or approves its design. The detailed audit contains the supporting links.

What “experimental” means

The project is producing candidates and recording design evidence so that educational value and physical usability can be evaluated. The earlier 856 meanings have production review records covering source/model identity, geometry, nominal medium constraints, rendered profiles and reproducibility. The later 42 have human Keep decisions and nonblocking BANA-informed comments. Package checks verify identities, exact file bytes and completeness; they do not upgrade the review history. These checks are valuable, but they do not establish that a learner can recognise or understand the graphic by touch.

Physical printing/embossing calibration, tactile proofreading, device/page fit and learner evaluation remain pending. Some current designs use large nominal canvases; a proof displayed on a screen does not demonstrate practical classroom size. No recognition rate, learning gain or claim of full accessibility certification is made.

The graphics intentionally contain no visible text or Braille. Names, keys, scales, colour explanations and model limitations must therefore be supplied through an appropriate external accessible teaching resource. This constraint can reduce the usefulness of flags, tables, graphs or puzzles if their essential information is not otherwise available.

Using the collection in an evaluation

For a selected item, first state what the learner should do or explain, identify relevant prior knowledge, choose the physical medium and provide the necessary key. Evaluate the produced material with appropriate teaching support. Record difficulties in orientation, tracing, distinguishing parts and understanding the intended relationship. Compare with an available real object, model or alternative representation where useful. These are proposed evaluation questions, not evaluations already completed.

Experimental status is not an exemption from accurate anatomy, honest provenance or clear teaching purpose. A poor or unnecessary item should be identified openly. The final selection reflects the human’s explicit decisions; the historical audit itself was an editorial assessment, not an automatic approval or deletion rule.

Appendix A — group purposes and origins

These explanatory groups follow the existing project topic framework, with the foundation targets shown separately. They are broad, sometimes overlapping groupings, not a new allocation of all 1251 meanings or counts of completed symbols. The final two groups explain reference material that is not commissioned teaching artwork.

Foundational tactile and relational concepts

Contents: Position, shape, size, quantity, part–whole relationships and following lines.
Intended purpose: Support prerequisite spatial language and tactile graphic reading through explicitly taught comparisons.
Source basis: The project scope records 83 foundation and 103 additional relational targets; these are not an extra 186 beyond the 1251.
Qualification: Curriculum relevance can be strong, but prerequisite sequencing and tactile learning need assessment.

Mathematics

Contents: Shapes, fractions, measurement, graphs, geometry and selected puzzles.
Intended purpose: Make quantities and spatial relationships available for exploration and explanation.
Source basis: APH/BTactile topic references plus independently specified mathematical constructions.
Qualification: Core diagrams have direct purposes. Extra puzzle silhouettes, fractals and conic constructions need a named lesson and progression.

Life science and anatomy

Contents: Organisms, cells, organs, skeletons, reproduction, genetics and ecological relationships.
Intended purpose: Trace structures and processes, compare models and connect form with function.
Source basis: APH-informed named topics supplemented by per-item scientific, specimen and anatomy references.
Qualification: Many direct KS1–A-level links; detailed scutes, brain parts or isolated fossil parts may be specialist. A flat image is not evidence of tactile recognition.

Physical science

Contents: Matter, atoms, molecules, forces, waves, optics and apparatus.
Intended purpose: Represent structures or relations that cannot conveniently be explored at actual scale.
Source basis: Topic references and declared mathematical/scientific models, with per-item source records.
Qualification: Separate introductory models from A-level content; define every tactile convention and use practical experiences where appropriate.

Earth and space

Contents: Landforms, weather, tectonics, stars, orbits and spacecraft.
Intended purpose: Explore large-scale relationships and physical processes.
Source basis: APH-informed topics and per-item scientific/geospatial/astronomical references.
Qualification: Earth-process diagrams often have clear purposes. Elaborate star chains, spacecraft configurations and projection details need selected courses or projects.

Geography and history

Contents: World/regional maps, routes, historical extents, flags and landmarks.
Intended purpose: Support location, map interpretation, spatial comparison and historical context.
Source basis: Public source catalogue subjects plus declared cartographic datasets and historical sources.
Qualification: The initial coverage was source-led. Local US lessons can be valid; they are not universal requirements. Dataset components and every flag detail are not automatically educational necessities.

Daily living and mobility

Contents: Objects, equipment, transport, room plans and routes.
Intended purpose: Connect language and spatial representations with familiar experiences.
Source basis: Foundational teaching targets and object/route topics in the source framework.
Qualification: A personalised access goal may be more relevant than a subject syllabus. Use real objects, models and orientation teaching; do not presume a silhouette is recognisable.

Art and music

Contents: Compositions, patterns and musical instruments.
Intended purpose: Support selected discussions of form or connect instrument shape with sound and use.
Source basis: Public topic references and independent geometric or object designs.
Qualification: These supplement making, listening and performing. Decorative source images are not automatically useful tactile lessons.

Health and recreation

Contents: Body awareness, foods, games, play spaces and equipment.
Intended purpose: Support chosen activities, discussion and part-to-whole understanding.
Source basis: Educational object/activity topics and appropriate per-item references.
Qualification: A static graphic does not demonstrate safe physical operation, movement, eating or game performance; name the actual task.

Community and culture

Contents: Community settings, cultural objects, celebrations and selected symbols.
Intended purpose: Discuss meanings within a relevant community, story or local syllabus.
Source basis: Source catalogue subjects with contextual references where selected.
Qualification: Do not infer meaning from shape alone or assume one tradition represents all learners. A specific cultural/RE objective is needed.

Alphabet and print references — not an artwork group

Contents: Historical catalogue references containing letters or print.
Intended purpose: Explain why catalogue coverage differs from the commissioned set.
Source basis: The existing topic framework explicitly marks these reference-only.
Qualification: Project graphics contain no visible text or Braille. This is not a Braille-literacy programme or a reason to commission letter outlines.

Production and 3D references — not extra meanings

Contents: Templates, devices, calibration material and three-dimensional reference objects.
Intended purpose: Support technical development and explain the distinction between resources and teaching concepts.
Source basis: Reference-only material in the existing topic framework.
Qualification: These are not additional completed 2D meanings. Nominal checks do not establish physical device calibration or readable output.
