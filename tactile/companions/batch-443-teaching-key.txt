PAIRED POLAR HEMISPHERES
LEFT is the NORTH-polar orthographic view (visible latitudes0to90degreesN); RIGHT is the SOUTH-polar orthographic view (latitudes0to90degreesS). They show the SAME Earth dataset through opposite pole-centred orthographic views, not two different planet identities or a time change. Both disks have radius150mm and use identical scale. Outer circles are hemisphere viewing limbs, NOT coasts, latitude areas or national borders. Coastline fragments stop at the equatorial limb without an invented horizon-closing coast. No administrative boundaries are printed. Only the selected coarse physical land/water-edge source is represented; it is not a current ice-shelf/physical-coast survey date or complete island catalogue.
Greenwich0degrees is UP in BOTH disks. To preserve actual handedness when viewed from opposite poles,90degreesE is LEFT in the north view but RIGHT in the south view.90degreesW is opposite and180degrees is downward. This is an explicitly declared view rotation/orientation, not an accidental mirror or reversal of geographic east. The dashed upward radial reference is Greenwich, not a country boundary or route. The larger central solid marker is the respective pole location: it does not mean that the North Pole is land or mark a city/lake. Land around the South Pole and the Arctic ocean context must be taught with this key; no water/land texture code is asserted.
Projection uses longitude relative to Greenwich: x=-sign*R*cos(latitude)*sin(longitude), screen y=-R*cos(latitude)*cos(longitude), sign=+1north and-1south. The respective pole is the origin. Geographic coast segments are clipped at the equator and densified by linear lon/lat interpolation to at most0.25degree increments before projection. This declares a piecewise-line approximation, not an independent maximum-error/cadence proof. Orthographic projection foreshortens toward the limb; this is not equal-area and apparent distance at the rim is not globe arc distance. Synthetic source dateline/pole closures are omitted transparently, with their raw source edges preserved.

LONGITUDE/MERIDIAN GLOBE PLAN
One equator-centred orthographic sphere, Greenwich central longitude, north upward. The three selected visible meridians have relative longitudes45degreesW,0degrees and45degreesE. Central Greenwich meridian is a vertical diameter. The west/east visible meridian arcs are exact SVG half-ellipses with horizontal semiaxisR*sin45degrees and vertical semiaxisR. Formula at latitudephi: x=R*sin(lambda)*cos(phi), y=-R*sin(phi). The selected values are independently introduced teaching values, not recovered unreadable source labels. No visible angle numbers or prime-meridian letters are printed.
Each printed arc is the FRONT VISIBLE SEMICIRCLE from North Pole to South Pole. A complete3D great circle consists of that meridian together with its opposite longitude180degrees away. The hidden half is not printed as a second solid arc; no dashed hidden-meridian convention is asserted. All meridians meet both poles intentionally, including the central line: that convergence is the taught relation, not a defect to erase or a separate physical joint. Pole markers are larger introduced location cues. The outer sphere limb is a viewing reference, not another selected longitude, a particle orbit or a coastline.
The dashed horizontal diameter is an equator reference; it is not a meridian or a latitude-comparison lesson. Apparent angles/gaps on the flattened view do not directly equal longitude angles on Earth. The two marked poles are not2countries, atom particles or raised-dot counts. The drawing has no country geography, north arrow, time-zone allocation or navigation bearing.
