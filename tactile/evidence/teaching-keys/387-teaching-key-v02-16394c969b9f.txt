1. Vasco da Gama: first India voyage, 1497–1499
Concept: edu.vasco-da-gama-route-map

One voyage is compared in two panels. The LEFT panel shows the selected outward relationship: Lisbon → Cape rounding region → Malindi → Calicut/Kozhikode. The RIGHT panel shows the return relationship: Calicut/Kozhikode → Malindi → Cape rounding region → Lisbon. Open arrowheads show each direction. Only the selected calls and their order are taught; intermediate calls, individual ships, dates within the journey and complete tracks are omitted.

Both panels are north-up. They use the same introduced equirectangular coordinate layout. The retained modern Natural Earth source references are generalised; this is not an exact historical coast or navigational chart. The deliberately shared drawn corridor is reversed between panels to compare the relationship, without an identical-track claim.

Each outline circle is a DISPLACED CITY CALLOUT, not a filled place point or an exact harbour location. A straight leader joins it to a selected nearby shore reference. In each panel the upper-left circle denotes Lisbon, the lower-middle/right circle Malindi, and the far-right upper circle Calicut/Kozhikode. Lisbon and Calicut callouts are 22 nominal mm west of their shore anchors; Malindi is 22 mm east. The right panel repeats these positions 500 mm farther right. Shore-anchor offsets from the retained city coordinates are approximately 5.99 mm, 5.50 mm and 1.70 mm respectively in the declared map plane. Cape rounding is a REGION on the route, not an additional invented port.

Only selected shore references are printed: the Atlantic European segment, northwest/southern/eastern African segments, a western Indian segment and the Madagascar outline. Mediterranean, northern/inner-sea, Arabian/Red-Sea/Suez, eastern Indian and other unselected shore details are omitted. Blank areas have no land/water classification. No invented bridge joins an omitted shoreline. The route's chosen maritime segments were checked against the retained generalised land source; detailed navigation and every island are outside this model.

Projection for each panel: x = 40 + 3.2 × (longitude + 35) mm, with 500 mm added in the right panel; y = 30 + 3.2 × (45 − latitude) mm. Source coordinates are longitude then latitude. This layout preserves neither true area nor metric distance.
