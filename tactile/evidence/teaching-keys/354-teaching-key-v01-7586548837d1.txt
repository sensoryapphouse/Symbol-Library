1. WHITE-CLOVER TRIFOLIATE COMPOUND LEAF
Orient the long petiole downward. Three separate rounded leaflet outlines lie above, left and right of one common petiole region. A short petiolule physically connects each leaflet base to that region; the line is connected, not merely nearby. Each leaflet contains a sparse central midrib ending inside its blade. Count THREE leaflets belonging to ONE compound leaf, not six shamrock lobes, three whole plants or a four-leaf clover. The nominal 60mm petiolules are lengthened/separated for tactile legibility relative to a natural small leaf, without changing the real connection pattern. Stipules, pale chevron colouring, minute margin texture and most venation are omitted. Rounded contours are independent educational proportions; source1402 does not establish taxon identity.

2. WHITE-ASH COMPOUND LEAF
Orient the terminal leaflet upward and the petiole downward. The petiole below the lowest opposite pair continues into one continuous central rachis. Three opposite pairs of separate leaflets attach along the rachis through short petiolules; one terminal leaflet attaches at the top. Count SEVEN leaflets in ONE mature Fraxinus americana teaching example. It is not seven independent leaves, seven meanings or a woody twig. Each leaflet shows its own connected midrib and two sparse lateral-vein cues. Their finite lines lie within the appropriate blade; the short lateral stalk approaches from outside and truly meets the blade base. Other veins, edge details and natural variability are omitted. Smooth oval, entire-edged leaflets with pointed ends are selected; this is not a diagnostic template for identifying an unknown plant. Real white-ash foliage is variable, and source1619 is not authenticated as that species.

3. SUGAR-MAPLE SIMPLE LEAF
Orient the petiole downward from the cordate, indented blade base. There is ONE continuous Acer saccharum blade, not a compound leaf. Count FIVE major palmate lobes: one central upper lobe, two large upper lateral lobes and a smaller basal pair. Four additionally declared coarse marginal teeth/notches are subordinate features, not extra lobes, detached leaflets or additional meanings. Five primary-vein cues diverge from the genuine petiole/blade junction and terminate inside the appropriate blade regions. Fine venation and natural leaf asymmetry/variability are omitted.
