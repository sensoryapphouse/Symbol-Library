Lunar appearances and solar eclipses — introduced teaching key
Batch 334. Companion to the three tagged tactile models. This text is essential to interpreting their nontext codes. It is not an observing timetable or proof of physical accessibility. Nominal page sizes are illustrative; calibration, touch, device/page fit, learner recognition and individual suitability remain pending.

1. Eight-phase lunar sequence
One Moon is shown repeatedly at eight different stages, not eight simultaneous Moons. Start with the field at the top, move clockwise, and return from the eighth field to the first:
1 new; 2 waxing crescent; 3 first quarter; 4 waxing gibbous; 5 full; 6 waning gibbous; 7 third quarter; 8 waning crescent; then new again.
The equal apparent-disc fields use a Northern-Hemisphere upright convention. The illuminated portion grows on the right while waxing and diminishes on the left while waning. A Southern-Hemisphere view can have a different orientation; that would be another viewing convention, not another Moon.
A circle gives the repeated apparent-disc field. Two short horizontal interior hatch strokes indicate the illuminated portion under this deliberately introduced tactile code. They are not lunar surface markings, photons, brightness units, line-dot counts or an intrinsic meaning of raised material. The new field has no illumination hatches. The full field has hatches spanning nearly the whole interior. The six intermediate fields have hatches on the appropriate illuminated side and a projected terminator ellipse or diameter.
For nominal clarity the printed intermediate terminator is only its middle segment, ending at vertical offsets plus/minus 0.70 of the apparent-disc radius. The deliberate gaps before the limb are not illuminated material leaking into the dark side. The mathematical complete terminator and illuminated domain remain defined in the metadata. The hatch strokes are inset strictly within that domain; hatch length/count is not a measured fraction or brightness scale.
The introduced apparent illuminated fractions are 0, (1−1/sqrt(2))/2, 1/2, (1+1/sqrt(2))/2, 1, (1+1/sqrt(2))/2, 1/2, (1−1/sqrt(2))/2. Half of the Moon's sphere is illuminated throughout; the visible fraction changes through viewing geometry. Ordinary lunar phases are not caused by Earth's shadow. This appearance collection contains no orbit inset and does not imply an eclipse at every new or full Moon. The 700 by 700 mm nominal field arrangement is not an approved device/page fit.

2. Central total-solar-eclipse apparent-disc transit
This separate model uses nine scenes in row-major order: top row left to right, middle row left to right, bottom row left to right.
1 before first contact; 2 first external contact; 3 partial ingress; 4 second internal contact, totality begins; 5 mid-totality; 6 third internal contact, totality ends; 7 partial egress; 8 fourth external contact; 9 after fourth contact.
The opaque foreground Moon reference moves horizontally from left to right across the Sun in this introduced zero-impact-parameter example. The apparent Moon radius is exactly 1.1 times the apparent Sun radius. The displayed radii are 26.4 and 24 mm, obtained by scaling the selected model's 13.2 and 12 mm by two. The nine Moon-to-Sun horizontal offsets in the displayed panels are −60, −50.4, −24, −2.4, 0, +2.4, +24, +50.4 and +60 mm. These are constructed positions, not contact times or a named observed event.
Only Sun contour outside the opaque Moon is printed. Hidden solar contour is omitted. Scenes 4, 5 and 6 have no printed Sun boundary; the Moon reference contour alone can have the same occulted appearance. Do not invent visible differences, a hidden solar circle, corona, diamond ring or an extra tangency dot to identify those three stages. Their declared scene positions and this external key supply the order. The visible solar remnant during ingress/egress is an occultation, not a lunar-phase crescent. A Moon reference contour outside the solar disc is a teaching reference, not a claim about its naked-eye visibility or illuminated phase. The 720 by 600 mm nominal collection has no approved physical fit.

3. Total-solar-eclipse shadow alignment
Read from left to right: Sun, intervening Moon, Earth. This is a separate spatial cross-section, not the observer-view apparent-disc sequence above and not an Earth-shadow lunar eclipse. Display radii and distances are introduced rather than astronomical scale.
The Sun, Moon and Earth reference-circle centres are (150,250), (342,250) and (440,250) mm; their display radii are 120, 24 and 60 mm. Two inner optical boundaries are the common external tangents of the Sun/Moon reference discs and bound the umbra. Two wider optical boundaries derive from opposite-side common internal tangents and bound the penumbral envelope.
Only the shadow-side pieces beginning at the true Moon tangencies are printed. Their incident back-projections to Sun tangencies are mathematical references, not printed connections. Do not mistake the lines for rods, spacecraft paths or solid cone walls. The umbra boundaries stop at their first intersections with the near Earth circle. The umbral apex is x=390 mm, beyond the near Earth surface at x=380 mm; the small near-face region intercepted by the umbra represents totality. This does not mean all Earth is dark.
In this selected construction the Earth projection lies inside the broader penumbral envelope. The penumbra boundary lines miss the Earth circle and extend to x=520 mm; do not read their ends as Earth contact points. Penumbra is partial solar obstruction outside the umbra, not total darkness. No occurrence at every new Moon, real-event prediction, calibrated cone size or physical material fill is asserted. The 560 by 500 mm nominal guide is not an approved print/device fit.
